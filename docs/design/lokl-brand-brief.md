# LOKL Fashion — Customer Experience Redesign
## Objective
Redesign the complete customer-facing LOKL fashion shopping experience to feel **premium, editorial, intentional and custom-designed**, rather than like an AI/vibe-coded e-commerce website.
The attached Quickee screenshots are the primary **design inspiration/reference** for the redesign. I particularly like how Quickee uses:
* Strong editorial typography
* Large, high-quality visual assets
* Intentional whitespace
* Curated content blocks
* Visually rich category discovery
* Brand/store discovery
* Fashion-led merchandising
* Strong promotional messaging
* Dynamic delivery ETA
* Try & Buy
* A streamlined Bag/Checkout experience
* Product pages that feel premium rather than templated
* Search that is designed as a discovery experience
* Carefully structured homepage sections
* Consistent visual hierarchy
* Conversion-focused CTAs
**Do not clone Quickee.** Use it as a benchmark for the level of design quality, visual hierarchy, merchandising and interaction design. LOKL must have its own visual identity and, most importantly, retain its **hyperlocal commerce proposition**.
---
# 1. Core LOKL Positioning
The experience should communicate:
**Fashion from stores around you.**
LOKL is not a generic e-commerce marketplace and should not look like one.
The key differentiators are:
* Nearby offline stores
* Local merchants
* Real local inventory
* Fast delivery
* City-specific discovery
* Try & Buy where available
* Easy returns
* Local store identity
* Curated fashion discovery
* Products sourced from actual nearby stores
The interface should make the user feel:
> "These are fashion products I can actually get from stores around me."
rather than:
> "This is another generic online fashion catalogue."
---
# 2. Overall Design Philosophy
Move away from the current AI-generated/vibe-coded appearance.
The new design should feel like a **professionally designed fashion-commerce product**, with every section appearing intentionally art-directed.
### Design principles
* Editorial rather than dashboard-like
* Premium rather than generic
* Visual rather than text-heavy
* Curated rather than overcrowded
* Strong typography
* Deliberate spacing
* Consistent grid system
* Strong image hierarchy
* High-quality product photography
* Minimal unnecessary UI decoration
* Clear CTAs
* Subtle animations/micro-interactions
* Strong conversion hierarchy
Avoid:
* Generic rounded-card-everything layouts
* Excessive gradients
* Random floating elements
* Excessive shadows
* Generic AI-generated illustrations
* Excessive pills
* Overuse of icons
* Repetitive section patterns
* Sections that look like automatically generated components
* Excessive text
* Generic "Shop Now" everywhere
The UI should feel **designed by a fashion-commerce product/design team**, not assembled from UI components.
---
# 3. Visual Assets
I will provide/upload the custom images myself.
The system should therefore be designed around **large, high-quality custom visual assets**.
Do not substitute these with generic stock images or automatically generated images.
The design should support:
* Full-width hero imagery
* Editorial banners
* Category imagery
* Store imagery
* Product imagery
* Brand imagery
* Lifestyle imagery
* Campaign imagery
* Promotional creatives
Image containers should be flexible enough that the experience still looks premium when different campaign creatives are uploaded.
---
# 4. Homepage Architecture
Create a rich, editorial homepage rather than a simple product grid.
However, **do not force the entire homepage to split into Women and Men.**
The preferred approach is:
### Primary discovery layer
**ALL | WOMEN | MEN**
This should be visually prominent but lightweight.
The user can immediately switch the merchandising context, while the default homepage remains a rich marketplace experience.
The homepage should dynamically adapt content based on:
* Location
* Gender preference/context
* Browsing behaviour
* Previous purchases
* Nearby inventory
* Merchant availability
* Trending products
* Current campaigns
* Time of day
* Delivery availability
---
# 5. Homepage Structure
Explore a structure inspired by modern fashion-commerce editorial merchandising:
### A. Location + Search Header
Prominently show:
**49 Mins**
or the actual dynamic delivery estimate.
Example:
> Delivering to Juhu
> **49 mins**
Location should remain editable.
Search should be highly visible and feel like a primary discovery mechanism.
Potential dynamic search prompts:
* "Search dresses"
* "Search sneakers"
* "Search something for tonight"
* "Search nearby stores"
* "What are you looking for?"
---
### B. Hero Section
Create a visually dominant editorial hero.
The hero should support:
* Custom campaign image
* Strong headline
* One-line supporting copy
* Primary CTA
* Optional secondary CTA
* Carousel support
The copy should feel fashion/editorial rather than generic e-commerce.
Examples of tone:
> Your city. Your style. Your stores.
> Fashion, closer than you think.
> Looks you'll love. Stores around you.
> Your neighbourhood just got fashionable.
The actual copy can be changed later.
Hero content should also be location-aware where relevant.
---
### C. Quick Gender Discovery
Immediately after the hero:
**Shop Women | Shop Men**
Use strong visual treatment rather than basic buttons.
This should be a discovery shortcut, not a completely separate homepage architecture.
---

# 7. Local / Hyperlocal Discovery
This is where LOKL must differentiate itself from Quickee.
Introduce sections such as:
### "Around You"
Products available from nearby stores.
### "Popular in [City]"
For example:
> Popular in Bhilai
### "Stores Near You"
Show actual nearby merchants.
### "Your Neighbourhood Picks"
Curated products from stores around the user's location.
### "Available in 49 mins"
Products that can actually reach the user within the current ETA.
### "Just Landed Nearby"
New inventory recently added by local stores.
The location should meaningfully affect merchandising rather than merely appearing in the header.
---
# 8. Shop by Store / Shop by Brand
I particularly want to explore a **Shop by Store** experience.
LOKL merchants should be capable of having a **Brand/Store identity tag** attached to products during product addition.
For example:
Product:
> Oversized Graphic T-Shirt
Store:
> XYZ Fashion Studio
or
> ABC Menswear
This allows LOKL to create:
### Shop by Store
Users can discover merchants rather than only products.
The store cards should include:
* Store name
* Store logo
* Store image
* Distance
* Delivery ETA
* Rating
* Number of products
* Optional "Verified Store" indicator
* Locality
For example:
> **Style Street**
> Sector 10, Bhilai
> 2.4 km away
> 35 min delivery
This should make the merchant feel like a real retail destination.
Do not make stores feel like anonymous sellers.

# 12. Dynamic ETA
Dynamic delivery ETA is a major LOKL advantage and should be surfaced throughout the experience.
Examples:
**49 mins**
or:
> Get it today by 9:48 AM
Use real-time/dynamic ETA wherever technically possible.
Show ETA on:
* Homepage
* Product cards
* Product page
* Store page
* Bag
* Checkout
The user should always understand **how quickly the product can reach them**.
---
# 13. Product Cards
Product cards should feel premium and editorial.
Each card can include:
* Product image
* Product name
* Store/brand name
* Price
* MRP
* Discount
* Delivery ETA
* Try & Buy indicator where applicable
* Wishlist
* Local/nearby indicator where useful
Avoid stuffing every piece of information into the card.
Prioritise:
**Image → Product → Price → Store/ETA → Action**
Product photography should remain the dominant element.

It should include:
### Product imagery

> **49 mins**

### Product information
* Product name
* Store/brand
* Price
* MRP
* Discount
* Coupon price
* Sizes
* Size chart

* Product details
### Store identity
Show:

* Store logo

### Try & Buy
Where the merchant supports it, make **Try & Buy a first-class experience**, not a buried feature.

**Try & Buy**
Try it at home before deciding.
Explain the rules clearly.
---
# 15. Bag + Checkout
One of the most important ideas from the reference is reducing friction between cart and checkout.
Do not create an unnecessarily fragmented:
Cart → Review Cart → Checkout → Payment
journey.
Explore a **single Bag/Checkout experience** where appropriate.
The Bag should contain:
* Products
* Quantity
* Offers
* Coupon
* Delivery ETA
* Try & Buy information
* Address
* Payment method
* Order summary
* Final CTA
The goal is:
**Add to Bag → minimal friction → Pay**
Reduce unnecessary steps.
Keep the CTA persistent where appropriate.
Example:
> ₹1,849
> **Proceed to Pay**
The experience should be conversion-focused without feeling aggressive.
---

# 18. Categories Experience
The Categories page should also follow the editorial philosophy.

**Categories**

**WOMEN | MEN**

---
# 19. Navigation
Retain a simple bottom navigation structure.
Suggested:
**Discover | Categories | Search | Bag | Profile**
The active state should be visually clear.
Bag should show a small item count.
Keep navigation minimal and consistent.
---
# 20. LOKL Visual Identity
The Quickee reference uses a distinctive yellow/black visual language.
LOKL should **not simply copy that identity**.
Retain and refine LOKL's own brand language.
Use:
* LOKL brand colours
* Strong black/white foundation
* Controlled accent colour
* Premium typography
* Editorial imagery
* Consistent spacing
* Strong visual hierarchy
The LOKL lightning mark can be used as a subtle brand motif across the experience.
Do not overuse it.
---
# 21. Local Commerce Must Remain Visible
At every major stage, users should understand that LOKL is about **nearby stores**.
Do not accidentally turn LOKL into a generic online fashion marketplace.
The local context should appear through:
* Location
* Delivery ETA
* Store identity
* Distance
* Locality
* Nearby inventory
* City-specific collections
* Local trends
* Store pages
* "Available near you"
* "Popular in [City]"
The experience should answer:
**What can I buy?**
**Who am I buying it from?**
**Where is it coming from?**
**When will I get it?**
without creating information overload.
---
# 22. Content & Copy Philosophy
Copy should be short, confident and fashion-forward.
Avoid generic AI-commerce language such as:
> "Discover our amazing collection of products curated just for you."
Instead use sharper copy:
> New season. Nearby stores.
> Looks for tonight.
> Trending around you.
> Your city's favourites.
> Fresh drops nearby.
> Fashion in minutes.
> From stores around the corner.
The copy should feel human and editorial.

* Product imagery

---
# 24. Personalisation
The homepage should not remain static.
Where sufficient data exists, dynamically prioritise:
* User's location
* Gender preference
* Previously viewed products
* Previously purchased categories
* Nearby stores
* Current inventory
* Trending products
* Delivery speed
* Price preferences
* Seasonal campaigns
However, personalisation should feel natural.
Do not expose the algorithm.
The experience should simply feel like:
> "LOKL knows what I might want."
---
# 25. Conversion Principles
The redesign should be measured against conversion, not only aesthetics.
Prioritise:
* Faster discovery
* Fewer clicks
* Clear pricing
* Clear availability
* Clear ETA
* Strong product imagery
* Strong store trust
* Easy Try & Buy
* Minimal checkout friction
* Relevant recommendations
* Local inventory confidence
Avoid adding UI purely because it looks good.
Every major component should have a reason to exist.
---
# 26. Responsive Behaviour
The primary experience is mobile-first.
The attached screenshots are primarily a reference for mobile interaction and visual hierarchy.
Ensure:
* Excellent mobile spacing
* Thumb-friendly controls
* Sticky CTAs where appropriate
* Fast image loading
* Smooth horizontal carousels
* Proper image aspect ratios
* No layout shifts
* Bottom navigation remains accessible
* Product cards remain visually strong at different screen sizes
The desktop experience should adapt the same design system rather than becoming a completely different product.
---

# 29. Final Design Goal
The final LOKL experience should feel like:
**Quick-commerce speed + fashion editorial discovery + local-store authenticity.**
It should look premium enough that users want to browse even when they did not come with a specific product in mind.
The user should be able to move naturally through:
**Discover → Explore → Find a product → Understand the store → See delivery ETA → Try & Buy → Add to Bag → Checkout**
with as little friction as possible.
Most importantly:
### Make LOKL feel like a real fashion product designed by a strong product/design team — not an AI-generated marketplace template.
The result should be **distinctly LOKL**, deeply local, city-aware, merchant-aware and conversion-focused, while matching the level of visual sophistication demonstrated in the attached Quickee references.
